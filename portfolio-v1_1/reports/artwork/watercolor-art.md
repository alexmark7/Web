# Watercolor landscape assets

Original artwork generated with the built-in image-generation tool. The user's supplied references and additional ink-wash landscape inspiration informed the composition; stock preview images are not included in the site.

## Display exports and loading

The current master paintings are kept in `assets/scenery/` under the project folder; the active seasonal edits are documented in [autumn-scenery.md](autumn-scenery.md). The website uses smaller transparent WebP exports in `images/optimized/`, rebuilt with `bash scripts/optimize-scenery.sh` from inside `portfolio-v1_1` (requires ImageMagick with WebP support). Earlier experiments remain documented below for provenance, but their unused image files were removed during cleanup.

- CSS `image-set()` selects normal or `@2x` exports for the display density. The 16 active normal-resolution paintings total 417,770 bytes, compared with 3,201,064 bytes for the 17 active source images (87% less). The high-resolution exports total 1,263,688 bytes (about 61% less).
- The mountain's three identical pigment washes are baked into one image, preserving their combined opacity while removing two repeated background paints in the browser.
- The active mountain export is `watercolor-mountains-defined-ridge`. A locally integrated image edit replaces the smeared rear shoulder with one continuous watercolor contour. The original main peaks and pixels outside the narrow ridge band remain unchanged, including the existing three pigment washes and connected slopes. See [the edit notes and prompts](autumn-scenery.md).
- The river export uses fewer vertical pixels to match its shallow on-screen proportions. Tiny hamlets and riverbank groves share a six-cell sprite: three hamlets, then three groves. Their independent CSS sizing and bank placement are preserved.
- Mountains, river, sun, clouds, and the footer cherry tree keep their separate responsive placement and animation. Combining these into one fixed painting would change their proportions or move the footer tree with the sky.
- The cherry tree starts fully below the viewport, then rises with root scroll progress. It travels 80% of its size plus 8px, using an ease-out curve that starts at the same faster pace but retains a gentle speed near the footer instead of flattening almost to a standstill. The native CSS curve and JavaScript fallback preserve fractional positions and share the same shape. It uses a native scroll timeline where supported and shares the existing animation-frame fallback elsewhere. Reduced motion places it in the document near the footer, below the initial viewport, without parallax. There is no footer-triggered slide or idle tree animation.
- `js/theme.js` warms the appropriate exports during idle time and waits for image decoding before switching from dark to light. An initial light-mode visit reveals the artwork together after decoding. Failed images do not block theme switching, and reduced-motion preferences suppress the reveal animation.
- Keep the export list in `js/theme.js` aligned with the active image URLs in `css/style.css` when adding artwork.
- Slow sun/cloud movement uses a small `0.1deg` rotation to avoid whole-pixel snapping in Firefox WebRender. Earlier isolated pixel comparisons passed at `0.01deg`, but the user's Firefox still showed stepping at roughly 60 frames per second. [Mozilla bug 2069657](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2069657) reports that `0.01deg` can still snap while `0.05deg` avoids it; `0.1deg` gives the workaround some margin. Firefox 159 screenshot comparisons at `0.1deg` distinguished all nine positions from 0 to 1px for both sun and clouds. The user confirmed smooth motion in the affected Firefox after this change. Reduced-motion mode does not use the rotation.
- `js/river-scene.js` combines the river, small village and bank details, pigment and forest into one `.watercolor-river` canvas. It paints after image decoding and when the viewport changes, caching the finished geometry; there is no idle canvas animation loop. The canvas follows the existing shared scroll transform and reduced-motion behavior. Its surface extends from the river to 4vh beyond the viewport bottom, so scrolling cannot reveal an empty strip.
- Woodland paint width follows the resolved mountain scale: 34% for a small rear bank row, 40% for the pale shore row and 48% for the nearer rows, with no vertical stretching. The rear row closes pale gaps where the winding riverbank meets the larger canopy. Every grove uses the flatter right-hand canopy of the source. A soft contour places the forest around the mountain's base, avoiding the old taller foothill section. Overlapping, offset and occasionally mirrored groves repeat downward to fill the entire foreground instead of leaving a finite band with blank paper beneath it. Horizontal tile spacing is 48% of paint width, so the 22% overlap exceeds even two mirrored wide edge fades (21%) and avoids pale vertical seams in the paper backing.
- The finished canvas has full CSS opacity on desktop and mobile. Composed tree interiors receive an opaque paper-tone backing before the mountain-base mask is applied; colour compensation preserves their appearance on the cream page. True gaps and the light outer pigment remain feathered, while scenery no longer shows through the dense forest.
- A few faint wisps of the already-loaded `watercolor-cloud-06` texture form pale mist over the woodland. Each receives a full elliptical feather, including the flatter underside, and is placed at a slightly different angle, width and height. Lower opacity and a brighter paper tint retain the tree colours instead of creating a dull flat band or a straight cutoff on the right. They are composited onto existing tree pixels after the opacity backing, before the mountain-base mask; the forest stays opaque and the river remains clear. Desktop uses three offset wisps and mobile uses two. The mist is painted into the same static canvas, with no additional artwork download or animation loop.
- The foreground cherry tree uses the solid-interior edit `watercolor-cherry-tree-solid` at full CSS opacity. The blossom and trunk paint blocks the scenery behind it, while empty branch gaps and feathered silhouette edges retain transparency. Its position follows scroll progress as described above. Footer columns reserve space to its left, with a smaller cropped tree on phones.
- The active `watercolor-river-forest-autumn` exports are 720px and 1440px wide, at 41,854 and 136,304 bytes respectively. A scattered minority of the broadleaf trees have warm autumn foliage among the green conifers. Theme preparation waits for the deferred painter and the completed canvas before revealing light mode. The original river inset, basin position, small detail scale and wide-screen reach are preserved internally in the combined painting. Outdated asynchronous resize requests cannot overwrite a newer scene.
- Cards, skill badges, social icons, buttons and nested case-study surfaces use their tints and borders without backdrop blur. Project, information, contact, CV and profile cards use a stronger warm-paper tint in light mode and a deeper night tint in dark mode to keep the scenery from competing with the text. All content cards, including the profile-picture frame, are free of backdrop blur. Panels keep a single restrained light-theme shadow. The navbar is transparent at scroll position zero. Its solid theme-matched background and thin bottom divider fade in together over 300ms when scrolling starts, and fade out again on returning to the top. It has no backdrop blur at any scroll position; reduced motion disables the fade.
